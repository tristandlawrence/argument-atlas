Debates  /  Should platforms be liable for user content?

Should social media platforms be held legally responsible for content posted by users?

Debate on whether platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube should face liability for user-generated content, or whether Section 230 protections should remain intact.

Arguments for

(3)

Algorithmic amplification of harmful content is a deliberate product decision

Recommendation engines actively select and amplify content that maximises watch time — that is an editorial decision, not passive hosting.

Fact Strong evidence

Moderation technology exists — liability would create the missing financial incentive

Platforms deploy sophisticated ML for spam and copyright already. What's missing is a financial reason to point it at harm.

Fact Strong evidence

Platforms profit from harmful content and should share liability

Outrage and conflict drive more engagement than measured discourse — and platforms profit directly through advertising.

Fact Moderate evidence

Arguments against

(3)

Section 230 created the conditions for the modern internet — removing it risks all of it

Without it, Wikipedia, Reddit and most social media couldn't exist in their current form. It's the legal foundation of the participatory web.

Fact Strong evidence

Liability would entrench large platforms by making compliance prohibitive for competitors

Meta and Google can afford compliance teams; a startup or cooperative network cannot. It would cement the incumbents critics want to constrain.

Fact Moderate evidence

Content creators, not hosts, should bear liability for what they publish

A postal service isn't liable for a defamatory letter it carries. Responsibility belongs to whoever chose to publish.

Proposal Moderate evidence
