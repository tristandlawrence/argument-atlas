1 · Naming & mark

Throughline is out. The replacement is Crux — the decisive point on which an argument turns. Short, ownable, and it names the thing the product is trying to find.

OPTION A
Argument Atlas

“Arguments worth having.”

Maps the terrain of a debate. Reads as a reference work. Two words and four syllables is the cost.

OPTION B
Debattic

“Arguments worth having.”

Ownable and distinct, but the portmanteau reads startup rather than civic institution.

OPTION C · new
Crux

“Find the crux.”

One syllable, plain English, no explanation needed. The mark is two claims crossing with the point of disagreement marked — which is literally the data model.

4 · Rating the connection, three ways

Once any claim can attach to any other claim, the link itself needs a quality signal — a true statement can be irrelevant to the claim it's filed under.

OPTION 1 · third signal
Persuasive?▲▼
Well made?▲▼
Relevant here?▲▼

One more thumb pair on the child card. Cheapest to build and consistent with what's there — but three questions per card is real voting fatigue, and the answer changes depending on which parent you're viewing it from.

OPTION 2 · on the link · recommended
Amplification is an editorial decision
SUPPORTS · DECISIVE
Tangential Relevant Decisive

The rating lives on the connector, not the card — which matches the data model, since the same claim can be decisive in one debate and tangential in another. Three named steps, no fourth thumb pair.

OPTION 3 · weight by thickness
Amplification is an editorial decision
Moderation technology already exists
Platforms profit from harm

No new control at all — connection strength is inferred from the child's own scores and drawn as line weight. Zero friction, but readers can't correct a link that's simply filed in the wrong place.
